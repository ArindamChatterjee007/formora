The V-Taper Workout: Build Broad Shoulders & a Small Waist

The "V-taper" is the illusion of width up top and a tight waist below — the single most impactful look for an aesthetic physique. You build it by growing three things and controlling a fourth.

The 4 levers of a V-taper

  • Side delts (shoulders) — the #1 width driver. Wide shoulders make everything below look smaller.
  • Lats (upper back) — flare from the armpits down; they create the taper.
  • Upper chest & traps — fill out the frame so width looks solid, not skinny.
  • Waist control — you can't spot-reduce, but staying lean and not over-training the obliques with heavy weighted side-bends keeps the waist tight.

The weekly split (3–5 days)

A simple upper-focused Push / Pull / Legs works well. Do it 3x/week (each once) as a beginner, or 5–6x/week (repeat push & pull) as you advance.

The exercises that actually build width

Lateral raises (the non-negotiable)

Most people's side delts are underdeveloped. Do 3–5 sets of 12–20 reps, 2–3× per week. Lighter weight, strict form, a slight lean away from the dumbbell, and control the lowering. Volume beats ego here.

Lat pulldowns & pull-ups (wide grip)

Drive the elbows down and slightly out; think about pulling with the armpits, not the biceps. 3–4 sets of 8–12.

Overhead press

Your main strength lift for shoulder mass. 4 sets of 5–8. Progress the weight over weeks.

Rows & face pulls

Thickness + healthy shoulders. Face pulls (3×15–20) also fix posture, which instantly improves how wide you look.

Progression rule: add a rep or a little weight almost every week. If you did 3×12 lateral raises with 8 kg this week, aim for 3×13 or 9 kg next week. Tiny, consistent overload is what changes your frame over months.

How long does it take?

A visible V-taper change takes 3–6 months of consistent training + staying reasonably lean (roughly 10–15% body fat for men). The shoulders and back respond faster than most muscles because they start undertrained.

FAQ

Can I get a V-taper without a small waist?

Width up top matters most, but a lean waist doubles the effect. You don't need visible abs — just avoid excess belly fat and don't add bulk to the obliques with heavy loaded side bends.

How many days per week?

3 days works. 4–5 is faster if you can recover. Side delts and lats tolerate frequent training, so hitting them 2–3×/week is ideal.
