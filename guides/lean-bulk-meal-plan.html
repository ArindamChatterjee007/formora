Lean-Bulk Meal Plan: Gain Muscle, Not Fat

A "lean bulk" means eating just enough of a surplus to build muscle while keeping fat gain slow. Done right, you add size and stay sharp. Done wrong (a "dirty bulk"), you spend the next season cutting it all off.

Step 1 — Calories: a small surplus

Find your maintenance calories (what keeps your weight stable), then add a small surplus — about +250–350 kcal/day. That's enough to fuel muscle, not so much that you balloon.

A quick estimate for maintenance: bodyweight (kg) × 30–33 if you're moderately active. Track your weight weekly — you want to gain roughly 0.25–0.5% of bodyweight per week. Faster than that is mostly fat.

Step 2 — Protein: the muscle input

Aim for 1.6–2.2 g of protein per kg of bodyweight per day. For a 75 kg person that's ~120–165 g. Spread it across 3–5 meals for a steady supply.

Step 3 — Carbs & fats: the fuel

  • Carbs power your training — keep them moderate-to-high, mostly around workouts (rice, oats, potatoes, fruit, roti).
  • Fats support hormones — don't go too low. ~0.6–1 g/kg (nuts, oils, eggs, dairy).
  • Fill the rest of your calories with carbs after protein and fat are set.

The lean-bulk golden rule: if the mirror is getting soft and the scale is jumping fast, cut your surplus in half. Slow and steady beats blowing up and cutting for months.

Veg & vegan lean bulk

Completely doable — lean on paneer, tofu, lentils, chickpeas, soya, Greek yogurt and a plant or whey protein. The only difference is you'll plan protein a bit more deliberately.

FAQ

How long should a lean bulk last?

Usually 3–6 months, then a short mini-cut if body fat creeps up. Beginners can often build muscle at maintenance or even a tiny deficit ("recomp").

Do I need supplements?

No — food comes first. Whey protein and creatine are the only two with strong evidence and both are optional conveniences, not requirements.
