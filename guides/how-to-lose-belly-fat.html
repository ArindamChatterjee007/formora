How to Lose Belly Fat: What Actually Works

There's more nonsense written about belly fat than almost any fitness topic. Here's the honest version — no teas, no "fat-burning" foods, no ab gadgets.

First, the hard truth: you can't spot-reduce

Doing 500 crunches won't burn the fat on your stomach. Your body loses fat from everywhere at once based on your overall calorie balance — the belly is often just the last place to go. So the goal isn't "ab exercises," it's getting your whole body leaner.

The one thing that matters: a calorie deficit

You lose fat when you eat slightly fewer calories than you burn. Everything else is a detail. A sensible target is a 300–500 calorie daily deficit, which drops roughly 0.5 kg of fat per week.

4 habits that make the deficit easy

  • Prioritise protein (1.6–2.2 g/kg) — it keeps you full and protects muscle while you lose fat.
  • Eat mostly whole foods — they're filling for fewer calories. Liquid calories (soda, juice, sugary coffee) are the silent killers.
  • Walk daily — 8,000–10,000 steps burns real calories without wrecking your appetite like intense cardio can.
  • Sleep 7–9 hours — poor sleep spikes hunger hormones and makes everything harder.

Should you train?

Yes — lift weights 2–4× a week. It preserves (or builds) muscle while you lose fat, so you end up lean and toned rather than "skinny-soft." Cardio helps burn calories but isn't required — the deficit is what matters.

Do abs still matter? Train your core for strength and posture, sure — but visible abs come from getting lean enough (roughly under 15% body fat for men, under 24% for women), not from doing more crunches.

FAQ

How long does it take to lose belly fat?

At ~0.5 kg/week, most people see a noticeably flatter stomach in 8–16 weeks, depending on where they start.

Is belly fat dangerous?

Excess visceral (deep belly) fat is linked to health risks, which is another reason getting leaner is worth it — not just for looks.

Do fat burners work?

No meaningfully. Save your money — the deficit does the work.
