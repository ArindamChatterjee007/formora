How to Build Muscle: The 4 Rules That Matter

Building muscle isn't complicated — it's just hard to do consistently. Ignore the noise; these four rules drive about 90% of your results.

Rule 1 — Progressive overload

Muscle grows when you give it a reason to. That reason is doing a little more over time — more weight, more reps, or better control. If your workouts look identical month after month, so will your body. Track your lifts and beat last week, even slightly.

Rule 2 — Eat enough protein

Protein is the raw material for muscle. Aim for 1.6–2.2 g per kg of bodyweight daily, spread across your meals. This is the single most important nutrition factor for muscle growth.

Rule 3 — Eat enough total food

Building muscle is easier in a slight calorie surplus (+250–350 kcal/day). You can build muscle at maintenance as a beginner ("recomp"), but a small surplus speeds it up. Don't confuse "surplus" with "eat everything" — a small one is plenty.

Rule 4 — Recover

Muscle is built while you rest, not while you train. Sleep 7–9 hours, don't train the same muscle to failure every single day, and take a lighter week every 6–10 weeks. Consistency over months beats intensity for a week.

How much muscle can you realistically build? A natural beginner might gain 0.5–1 kg of muscle per month early on; that slows as you advance. It's a marathon — but the first year is the most rewarding.

What doesn't matter as much as you think

  • The "perfect" program — any sensible plan followed consistently beats the "optimal" plan you quit.
  • Supplements — food first. Creatine and whey are the only two worth considering, and both are optional.
  • Training to failure every set — leaving 1–2 reps in the tank lets you do more quality volume.

FAQ

Can I build muscle and lose fat at the same time?

Yes — especially as a beginner or if returning after a break. Keep protein high and calories near maintenance.

How often should I train each muscle?

2–3 times per week per muscle is the sweet spot for growth.
