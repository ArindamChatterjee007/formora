Beginner Gym Workout Plan: Your First 3 Months

New to the gym? Don't overthink it. A simple full-body routine done 3× a week will build more muscle and strength in your first months than any complicated "bro split." Here's exactly what to do.

Why full-body 3× a week?

As a beginner, your muscles recover fast and respond to almost any stimulus. Hitting each muscle 3× a week (instead of once) means more practice and faster progress. Keep it simple and show up consistently — that's 90% of it.

The routine (do it Mon / Wed / Fri)

Rest 60–120 seconds between sets. The whole session takes about 45 minutes.

The golden rule: progressive overload

Each week, try to do a little more than last week — one more rep, or 2.5 kg more. Write it down (or let an app track it). This steady progression is what actually builds muscle. When you can hit the top of the rep range on all sets, add weight.

Beginner mistakes to avoid: ego-lifting with bad form, program-hopping every 2 weeks, skipping legs, and worrying about supplements before you've nailed consistency and sleep.

What about nutrition?

To build muscle, eat enough protein (~1.6–2.2 g/kg) and don't crash-diet. To lose fat at the same time (very doable for beginners), keep protein high and calories at maintenance or a small deficit. See our lean-bulk meal plan.

FAQ

How many days a week should a beginner train?

3 full-body days is the sweet spot. It's enough to progress fast and easy to stay consistent with.

How long before I see results?

Strength jumps in the first 2–4 weeks ("newbie gains"); visible muscle in 8–12 weeks with consistency.

Free weights or machines?

Both work. Machines are great for learning safely; free weights build more coordination. Use whatever lets you progress with good form.
